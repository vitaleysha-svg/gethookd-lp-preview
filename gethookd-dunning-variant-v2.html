The answer is better than you expected, and it is the argument for the whole rebuild. You were right that day 1 is the highest. But across the trial sequence opens only fall 24% (39.1% to 29.6%) while clicks fall 97% (1.72% to 0.05%).

Emails 2 through 5 were opened 7,649 times and 7,529 of those people did nothing. That is not a dead channel, it is a wasted one. A third of the audience is still opening on day 5. They read it and there is nothing in it worth clicking, because the ask is still "update your card" and there is still no offer until email 3.

If emails 2 to 5 merely clicked at email 1's rate we would have had 391 clicks instead of 120. The audience is present. The copy is the constraint.

2. Does this collide with the Registered No-Card nudge?

Not simultaneously. Sequentially, nobody has ever checked. Two separate answers.

The good news, structurally: "Registered No-Card — Trial Nudge" triggers on gethookd_status changing to "User Registered!" browser_verified. Churnkey dunning triggers on a card that failed. Those are mutually exclusive states: with no card on file there is no charge to decline. A contact cannot legitimately be in both at the same moment.

The gap: our own wiring map lists that workflow, in writing, as "not yet audited". Its step timing and duration are not captured anywhere, and the GHL API 404s on workflow config, so the only way to confirm the sequential case (register, get nudged, add a card, trial charge fails on day 7, dunning starts while the nudge is still running) is a human reading it in the builder. I cannot verify non-overlap and I am not going to assert it.
Separate finding worth more than the overlap question. That workflow triggers on gethookd_status. Our own rule, locked 2026-06-01, is that only the five Boris-verified custom fields may be used for GHL triggers and gethookd_status is explicitly excluded because it is unreliable. So the no-card nudge is enrolling people off a field we decided not to trust. That is a live defect independent of dunning, and it belongs in the same builder session.

The safe guard to add regardless: a suppression check at the top of the dunning sequence. If in_sequence is set or the contact is enrolled in the no-card nudge, hold the dunning send by 24 hours. Cheap, and it makes the question moot without waiting for the audit.

Why five consecutive days is the right window, not a preference. The recovery-timing analysis on file says 65% of recoveries land by day 2, 95% by day 6, and zero after day 15. The current sequences spend emails 4 and 5 in a window where recovery has already stopped happening: on paid, emails 4 and 5 sent 1,210 times over six months for 8 clicks and 2 recoveries. A five-day sequence sits exactly on top of the window where money actually comes back.

The day-28 email is the one exception worth arguing about. It sends the cancellation warning, and on trial it produced 541 opens and one click. If you keep it, keep it as a separate day-28 final notice outside the five-day block, not as email 5.

5. The five emails

Locked voice applied: proper capitalisation, one-sentence positive opener, features named with the benefit inline and tied to time or money, one light self-deprecation at most, PS with a specific deliverable offer, and the signoff with title.

One deliberate deviation from the cancel-recovery spec. That spec says one CTA per email and the CTA is always Reply. Dunning has a different job: the card has to get fixed. So these carry a payment button plus the reply invitation. Everything else in the spec is followed. Flagging it rather than quietly breaking the rule.
