The failed-payment battlefield — still the biggest untouched lever ~27% of real payer churn is failed payments, not decisions — no cancel flow touches them. The August spike we traced (trial→annual first-charges bouncing, $229–$999) keeps this elevated: $7,363/mo lost this period on card failures alone. The lever is dunning: fallback-to-monthly offer, split-pay, or card pre-validation on annual trials. Churnkey Payment Recovery runs (~25% recovery) but isn't tuned for big-ticket annual failures.
Why the two views must stay separate Churnkey's ~80% "churn" (Cancel Flow view) = share of flow enterers who completed a cancel — a save-flow efficiency metric. Stripe's 11.3% = share of the entire paying base that ended — the business metric. Same word, different denominators. Layered, each validates the other: real churn falling + flow saves rising = the same story from two angles.

Bottom line — did real churn decrease?

Yes — monotonically, while the base grows
  • Real paying churn: 12.2% → 11.9% → 11.3% (three periods down), while paying base grew 5,161 → 5,666 and MRR $206,803 → $246,421.
  • Trial → paid conversion: 27.0% baseline → 32.5% this period.
  • ~27% of churn is failed payments — the flow can't reach it; dunning is the next lever.
Asks: (1) treat payment recovery as its own workstream (fallback-to-monthly on annual-trial failures); (2) keep both views layered — flow performance vs business truth.
