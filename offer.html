The offer

What we are selling

Stop paying to find out an ad does not work.

We read your account against 13 million live Meta ads and tell you what to test next, with the ad already running that proves it.

Why this is the positioning

Price is not the thing we are selling, so it should not be the thing we lead with. A business spending $1M a month does not weigh $499. They weigh what happens to the next $30,000 they put behind a creative. That is the number we move, and it is the only number worth talking about first. Lead with the price and we invite a comparison against a strategist's salary, which is a conversation about cost. Lead with the spend we protect and the price stops being the question.

How it runsFrom the first email to the weekly rhythm.

1The invitationDay 0

A short email asking for 30 minutes. The offer is never in the email, because an offer in writing gets compared and an offer on a call gets discussed.

  • You get a note saying we are putting five customers on something not launched yet, and you are one of the five.
  • No pricing, no deck, no attachment.
  • One question: 30 minutes this week?
2The onboarding call60 minutes

We find the one bottleneck in your account and agree the number we are moving. We gather on this call, we do not diagnose.

  • 10 min. What you sell, who buys it, and what you actually look at when you decide the next ad to make. Most people cannot answer that last one, and that alone is usually the finding.
  • 10 min. Spend, how many creatives you test a week, and how many of those are genuinely new concepts rather than variations. Target CPA and ROAS, and how far off you are.
  • 15 min. What has worked and your best guess why. What you tested that failed. What you keep meaning to test and never get to. This matters most for established brands, because cookie-cutter advice you have already tried is worthless.
  • 10 min. The one thing you would fix this month. Who makes your creative and how long one concept takes. Which competitors you watch. How fast you can get a static live once you have it.
  • 5 min. What to send today, when the first read lands, and your Slack invite.
3You send your numbersSame day

Until the ad-account connection ships you export it yourself. Instructions come with screenshots and take about five minutes the first time, under a minute after that.

  • Ads Manager export, last 30 days, at ad level, with the exact columns we need. Then a second export of the 30 days before it, because the comparison is where the signal is.
  • The creative, or a link to it, for your top 10 by spend and your 5 worst.
  • Your target cost per purchase and ROAS.
  • The 3 to 5 competitors you already watch.
4The first analysisBack in 1 to 2 business days

Your numbers say where the break is. The market says what to do about it. In that order.

  • Read the account. Every ad marked: hook problem, body problem, offer or page problem, or fine.
  • Count concept diversity. How many genuinely different ideas you have tested, not how many ads. This is the most common finding: one idea reworded twenty times, counted as twenty tests.
  • Check awareness coverage. Which levels of buyer you talk to, and which you never touch.
  • Run the market side. Winners in your niche, what your competitors launched recently, and the angles that exist in your market that you have never run.
  • Pick the moves and name the live ad behind each one.
5Your weekly read, and three staticsEvery week

One page, in Slack, so you can reply to a specific line instead of starting an email thread.

  • The one thing to do this week, in a sentence.
  • What to kill, with the number that condemns it.
  • What to feed, with the spend to move it to.
  • What to test next: the angle, the live ad that proves it with its link and how long it has been running, and why it applies to you.
  • Three statics built off that winner, with the source ad attached.
  • What changed on the number we agreed, since last week.
6Two calls a month, and Slack in betweenOngoing

Thirty minutes each, with the person who did the read. Not a product demo and not a success manager working from notes.

  • Walk through the read and argue with it.
  • Look at anything you launched since we last spoke.
  • Decide what is being tested before the next call.
  • Between calls: send a creative before you launch it and get a straight read, same day.
