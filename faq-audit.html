GetHookd · Variation C · FAQ audit

Are the FAQs actually built from the calls?

Alex asked whether the FAQ is grounded in real call transcripts and what should be added. Answer: six of the ten are grounded, one has no support at all, and the four most common objections customers actually raise are missing.

01 · Method

Every objection, blocker and gap recorded across 52 logged customer calls, 29 May to 24 August, was pulled from the call log and counted by theme. Each of C’s ten FAQ questions was then matched against that frequency data. The counts below are mentions across the corpus, not calls, so one call can contribute several.

The headline result The first question on the FAQ, “Why pay for this when Meta’s ad library is free?”, appears zero times across 52 calls. Meanwhile plan gating, the single most common thing customers get stuck on at 154 mentions, has no question at all. The page leads with an objection nobody has raised and omits the one everybody raises.
One honest caveat before anyone cuts that question The call log records customers, people who already paid and booked onboarding. They would never raise “why pay when the library is free”, because they already answered it by buying. This corpus is strong evidence for what confuses buyers after purchase and weak evidence for what stops them before it. So the recommendation is to demote that question, not delete it. Mladen’s review reached the same conclusion independently and moved it to position five.

03 · Four questions to add, in frequency order

Which plan do I actually need?

154 mentions · the most common blocker in the corpus

Everything in the library, the filters, boards and Brand Spy works on every plan. Clone Ads starts on Pro. Connecting your AI agent starts on Team. If the AI connection is the reason you are here, start on Team, because Starter and Pro cannot do it.

Gijs, 1 Jul: “MCP is Team-plan-only ($79); he’s on Pro ($49) and thinks Pro is already a little expensive. The one feature mapping to his goal is gated behind an upgrade he is resistant to.” Same pattern logged for Anmol, Tommy Siu and Gustav Paal.

What if my niche or my competitor is not in there?

55 mentions for the extension, 36 for country coverage

Install the free Chrome extension, open Facebook’s Ad Library, and save any ad. That brand and its ads get pulled into GetHookd and become searchable. This is the standard fix and it is what we do on onboarding calls. Coverage is thinnest in smaller countries and in service or info niches, so if that is you, the extension is the route.

Nellie blocked on South Africa. Cubbage could not find his niche or his competitor. Damon Lye, 26 Aug, blocked on Singapore with the extension given as the workaround. Ruben and Quinten Gish both hit thin service and info coverage.

What do credits cost, and what uses them?

31 mentions · no price list exists anywhere in the product

Needs the real figures before publishing. From the call log: Clone Ads is 2 credits per variation and Brand Spy is 2 credits per brand. Everything else needs confirming with the dev team.

Tommy Siu: “no per-feature credit price list anywhere.” Jake at TrueMed: “credit costs unclear for MCP calls.” Reshma Patel: “per-action credit costs not surfaced.” Christian Törnsten: credit-cost sensitivity on Brand Spy.

Is this Meta only?

5 mentions · low frequency, but it is a first-minute confusion

Yes. Facebook and Instagram ads today. TikTok is not in the library.

Beth thought the platform was “mainly TikTok”, could not find Meta, filtered channels and still saw TikTok URLs. Kyle Kerr flagged that the filters lack TikTok versus what he had read online.

04 · What the existing ten get right

Six of the ten map cleanly onto real, frequent objections and should not change: coverage in my niche, needing to be technical, what can I ask it, how do I know the numbers are real, will I still get charged, and how this differs from Foreplay and AdSpy. The three questions covering AI setup are correctly weighted, since that is the top theme in the corpus by a wide margin.

“How current is the data” and “I am just starting out” are both weaker on frequency but earn their place for other reasons. The first is the data-trust theme, which shows up as accuracy complaints rather than as a question. The second qualifies the buyer, which is what Alex asked for on 14 August.
