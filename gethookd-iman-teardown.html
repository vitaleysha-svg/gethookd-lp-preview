3. The one that decides everything: cost of goods

This is the constraint hiding under Matt's feedback, and it is worth stating plainly before choosing a fix.

Not one of Iman's six VIP bonuses is a scheduled human hour. Deep-dive sessions are one-to-many. Case studies are a document. Resources are a Notion board. The MacBooks and the trip are a lottery with a fixed cost regardless of how many people buy. Higgsfield is a sponsor's product. The "dedicated concierge" is a shared support queue with a nice name. That is precisely why $15 works. His marginal cost per additional VIP is close to zero.

Our core deliverable is 60 minutes of Vitaley. At $15 with fifteen seats a week that is $225 a week against a human hour, which is nonsense. Our price is not high because we are greedy. It is high because we put a person in the box.

So we cannot simply copy his price. But that 38% is the reason we should not simply delete the call either. It is the active ingredient. The question is who gets it and when.

4. The recommendation

Three ways to go. I am recommending the third and I will say why the other two lose.

MoveWhy not
ACopy him exactly. Kill the call, make everything one-to-many, price at $15.Removes the active ingredient to make the price cheaper. Our own log says the human is what produces the aha in 38% of calls. We would be optimising the wrapper by throwing out the thing that works.
BKeep the $97 with the call, just borrow his presentation.Fixes Matt's complaint but leaves the real problem: a $97 second purchase ninety seconds after the first one. Most people will not take it, so most people never activate, so churn is untouched.
C. Split it, and sequence the human.

The thank-you page sells $15, not $97. Two columns, exactly his layout, red X's and all. The $15 tier is everything in the current package that costs us nothing to duplicate: the twelve master prompts, the filter recipe card, MCP unlocked for 14 days, the 200 credits, the competitor teardowns, the scripts and landing page pack, the credit burn plan. All assets. No calendar. No ceiling. Anyone can buy it, and buying it is what forces activation.

The 60-minute call stops being an upsell and becomes a rescue. Offer it at day 3, at $97, only to the people who bought the $15 and still have not connected the agent or cloned an ad. That is the segment that genuinely needs a human, and it means Vitaley's fifteen weekly seats go to the people who demonstrably need them instead of whoever clicked first.

Why this is better than either alternative: it gets the impulse price and the volume, it keeps the human where it has the most leverage, it removes the seat ceiling from the main offer, and it turns my "honest risk" note in the current doc from a limitation into the design.

6. What NOT to copy

  • The income claim. "$373/Day" works for a free event with a disclaimer page. We are a SaaS with a real product and named customers, and a specific daily revenue number in a paid upsell is a liability we do not need.
  • The giveaway. MacBooks and a trip work when the buyer is a lottery-minded beginner and the event is the product. Ours would attract exactly the segment our call log already flags as the worst fit.
  • Having no guarantee. He can skip it at $15. Ours is the best thing in the package and it doubles as the retention mechanism. Keep it, even at $15.
  • The countdown theatre. His is tied to a real event date. Ours would be a per-user timer, which our buyers, who are ad people, will spot instantly.
