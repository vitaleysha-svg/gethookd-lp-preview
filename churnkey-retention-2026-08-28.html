Why the two reports must stay separate Churnkey's 81% "churn" = share of flow enterers who completed a cancel decision — a save-flow efficiency metric. Stripe's 10.9% = share of the entire paying base that actually ended — the business metric. Same word, different denominators; merging them into one table is how numbers stop making sense. Layered like this, each validates the other.

Bottom line — the answer to "did real churn decrease?"

The verdict
  • Real paying churn is DOWN vs pre-relaunch: 12.2% baseline → 10.9% this period (-1.2 pts).
  • Trial → paid conversion: 27.0% baseline → 31.9% this period.
  • The retention gains in the flow report are real but partial: the flow only touches voluntary leavers who engage it; ~28% of payer churn is failed payments no flow can reach.
Asks: (1) treat payment recovery (dunning) as its own workstream — it's ~a third of real churn; (2) keep both reports layered: Churnkey = flow performance, Stripe = business truth.
Method (audited 2026-08-28, Sigma-confirmed 99.6%). Enumerated from live subscription objects bounded by current_period_end ≥ Jun 19 (no event-retention limit). Paid churn = subscription ended in window AND ≥1 invoice with status=paid and amount_paid>0 across FULL invoice history (the latest-invoice shortcut undercounts ~42% and is never used). Trial churn = ended ≤ trial_end+1h. Never-charged post-trial tracked separately (558 this period). Voluntary vs failed-payment = final invoice collected vs not. Base = payers active at window start (started paying before it, ended after it). MRR = gross plan value, monthly-normalized; discounts not netted out. Windows in UTC, identical to the Churnkey report.
Source. Stripe LIVE, restricted read-only key, pulled 2026-08-28. Current period is 14 days including today — cancels decided but not yet effective appear in later windows (end-of-term lag).
