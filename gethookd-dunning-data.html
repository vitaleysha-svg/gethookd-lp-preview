1. The number that reframes the whole exercise

Of 2,515 recoveries in six months, 67 are attributed to the email sequence. The other 2,448 happened because Stripe retried the card and it worked. We sent 44,275 emails to earn 67 recoveries, which is one per 660 sends.

This matters for how you test. If you A/B test and measure total recovery rate, you will measure Stripe's retry engine with a rounding error of email on top, and every test will come back flat. The email can only be judged on the metric the email controls, which is the click.

2. Opens are fine. Clicks are the failure.

37.1% of 44,275 emails were opened. 1.01% were clicked. Deliverability is healthy, the subject lines work, and then the body loses them.

insufficient_funds is 52.5% of every failed payment and $347,470 of attempted charges. Their card is not broken. Their account is empty. Every email in both sequences opens by asking them to "update the card on file", which is advice for a problem they do not have. Meanwhile genuine card-data failures (wrong number, expired, lost, stolen, invalid account) are only about 5.3% of the total.

So the primary ask is aimed at one twentieth of the audience, and the half of the audience that is simply short on cash this week gets no path at all. That is what the offer is for, and that is why it belongs on email 1.

6,532 trials failed their first charge and never recovered. That is roughly 1,090 a month of people who wanted to become customers and whose payment did not go through. The paid blueprint recovers 47.4%; the trial one recovers 13.0%. Same emails, three and a half times worse.

Why this is also the right place to run the test. The trial blueprint sends about 1,790 first emails a month against the paid blueprint's 730. Any A/B test on paid volume alone would take most of a year to read. See §6.

5. Ten SMS steps are configured on these two blueprints and every one is switched off

Both blueprints carry a full five-step SMS sequence with a magic-link body already written, all enabled: false. With email clicking at 1.01%, an untried channel that lands on a phone with a one-tap link is the most obvious unexplored lever in the account. It is not part of the A/B test and should not be bundled into it, but it belongs on the list.

Constraint on file from Mladen (call 2026-09-02): Churnkey smart retries were tried and hurt us, 10 to 15 attempts over 8 weeks dropped our issuer ratings. So retry count stays where it is. SMS is a different mechanism and does not touch that.

6. The A/B test, designed honestly

What we can and cannot detect at real volumes, at 80% power and 95% confidence, testing email 1's click rate:

Arm baselineRelative lift to detectNeeded per armTime to read

Trial email 1, 1.72%+100% (to 3.4%)1,3171.5 months
Paid email 1, 1.91%+50%3,96510.9 months
Paid email 1, 1.91%+100%1,1843.2 months
Two conclusions fall straight out of that table.

One: run it on the trial blueprint. It has 2.4 times the volume, the worse recovery rate, and the bigger dollar leak. A test on paid volume cannot be read inside a quarter.

Two: the variant has to be a big swing, not a polish. Anything under a 50% relative lift is undetectable in a useful timeframe. So we are not testing a subject line or a button colour. We are testing a different email with a different ask, and if it only nudges things we will correctly conclude it was not worth shipping.

The test

WhereTrial Users blueprint, email 1 (day 0) only. Everything downstream held constant.
Primary metricClick rate on email 1. Not total recovery rate, because 97.3% of recovery is Stripe's retry engine and would drown the signal.

Split50/50. Churnkey's native A/B feature covers cancel flows only and list_ab_tests returns empty, so this needs a cloned blueprint plus an audience filter to split traffic. to confirm with Churnkey support
DurationSix weeks minimum, and stop at a pre-committed date rather than when it first looks good.
HypothesisNaming the real decline reason and offering a smaller amount, rather than asking everyone to update a card that works, at least doubles click rate on email 1.

7. The two emails

CONTROL, live today

Payment 💳 failure for Gethookd.ai.
Hey {{FIRST_NAME|there}} —

Just letting you know that we're having trouble completing your most recent payment.

👇 Mind updating the {{CARD_BRAND}} card on file ({{LAST_4}})? We'd hate for you to lose out on your subscription!
Update Your Card Now

What is wrong with it: "just letting you know" carries no stakes. "Mind updating" is a favour, not an instruction. "We'd hate for you to lose out" is about us. It never says what they lose or when. And the single ask, update your card, is the wrong instruction for the 52.5% whose card is fine and whose balance is not.

VARIANT, to test

Your GetHookd payment didn't go through
{{FIRST_NAME|Hey}} —

Your card came back insufficient funds, so the charge didn't land. Nothing's wrong with the card itself.

Your account stays live for 28 days, then it closes and your saved ads, boards and Brand Spy history go with it.

Two ways to sort it in about ten seconds:

1. Short this week? Take 50% off your next two months and we'll run it now at the lower amount.
Charge me the lower amount
2. Card's fine, just retry it.
Retry my card

Either one takes one tap, no login.

Reply to this and it comes to me directly.

Vitaley
Head of Customer Success, GetHookd

What changed and why: the real decline reason instead of a vague apology; a concrete loss with a date attached; the discount as the primary path because half the list is short on cash, not broken on card; two named options instead of one wrong one; a real human on the reply line; and the offer moved onto the email that gets 61% of the clicks.

Merge-field note: the variant reads best when the decline reason is dynamic. If Churnkey cannot merge failReason into the body, the fallback is two audience-filtered variants, one for insufficient_funds and one for card-data failures, which is stronger anyway. to confirm with Churnkey support

Why the order matters. If we A/B test the current email 1 against a variant while the offer is still misconfigured, we spend six weeks measuring a version of the sequence we already know is wrong, and whatever we learn will be relitigated the moment fixes 1 and 2 ship. Fix the wiring, let it settle for a fortnight, then test copy against a correctly wired baseline.
