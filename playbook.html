We answer the one question every advertiser is stuck on: what do I test next. Answered from their own numbers plus the whole market, not from copying the one competitor they already watch. The software finds what is working. The service tells them which of it applies to them, and in what order.

The six stages

Outreach to weekly delivery
1. OutreachDay 0
Email 10 agency-plan users with a low health score who have paid for months. Not a pitch, an invitation: we are building a creative strategy layer on top of GetHookd, you are one of five people getting it free, and I want your feedback more than your money. Ask for one hour.
Why these people: they already pay, they already use it, and if it works on them it works on the ones we will charge. Alex approved free for the first five so the pressure comes off the first runs.
2. Onboarding call60 minutes
Understand their business and find the bottleneck. The questions are below and the order matters. Do not diagnose on this call, gather. The one thing to leave them with: give me a day or two and you get a written game plan.
3. They send dataSame day
Until the ad account connection ships, they export it themselves. Ask for:
  • Ads Manager report, last 30 days, at ad level: ad name, spend, results, CPA, ROAS, CTR all, CTR link, CPC, CPM, frequency, and for videos hook rate and hold rate
  • The creative itself, or a link to it, for the top 10 by spend and the 5 worst
  • Their target CPA and ROAS
  • The 3 to 5 competitors they already watch
4. The analysis2-3 hours
Their numbers tell us where the break is. The GetHookd library tells us what to do about it. In order:
  • Read the account against the thresholds in the next section. Mark every ad: hook problem, body problem, offer or page problem, or fine.
  • Check concept diversity. Count how many genuinely different ideas they have tested, not how many ads. This is the most common finding.
  • Check awareness coverage. Which levels they run and which they never touch.
  • Run the market side in GetHookd: winners in their niche, what their competitors launched recently, which angles exist in the market that they have never run.
  • Pick the five moves and name the reference ad behind each one.
5. The game planBack in 1-2 days
One page plus a Loom. Structure: here is what your numbers say, here is the single biggest gap, here are five things to make next, each with the live ad that proves the angle works and what to keep from it. End with what we will measure next week.
6. Weekly1-1.5 hrs
They send the new export. We send the Monday note in Slack: what moved, what to kill, what the market did, one thing to make this week. Every two weeks that becomes the full "make these five" list. Calls twice a month, more by package.

The onboarding call, question by question

Gabriel's order: research first, production second
Block 1: what they sell and to whom (10 min)
  1. Who is the customer you are targeting, and why that person?
  2. What is the promise you are making to them?
  3. When you decide what ad to make next, what are you actually looking at? Where does that information come from?
Gabriel: start with market research and the source of truth. What avatar, why that avatar, what hypothesis sits behind the selling point, and what information they use to decide what to create. Most people cannot answer the third one, and that alone is the finding.
Block 2: volume and money (10 min)
  1. How much are you spending a month right now?
  2. How many creatives do you test a week, and how many of those are genuinely new concepts rather than variations?
  3. What is your target CPA and ROAS, and how far off are you?
Gabriel: ecom is a game of testing, so net new concepts per week is the number that matters. His own team tests 10 net new concepts a day. Then how far they are from their CPA and ROAS target, which is different for every brand.
Block 3: what they have already tried (15 min)
  1. What has worked so far, and what is your best guess why?
  2. What have you tested that failed?
  3. What do you keep meaning to test and never get to?
Gabriel, on established brands: the biggest letdown from consultants was cookie-cutter advice, because 99% of it was something they had already tested. For bigger brands, understanding what they have tested over the last months is the whole job before you open your mouth.
Block 4: the bottleneck, in their words (10 min)
  1. If you could fix one thing about your ads this month, what would it be?
  2. Who makes the creative, and how long does one new concept take you?
  3. Which competitors do you watch?
Block 5: close (5 min)
  1. Here is exactly what I need you to send me today.
  2. You get the written game plan in one to two days.
  3. Then we do this every week, and here is the Slack channel.

The numbers cheat sheet

Gabriel's thresholds, US traffic
MetricGoodWhat it tells you
Cost per click~$1, US $1.50-$2 okCheap attention. High CPC with a decent CTR usually means audience or CPM, not creative.
CTR allabove 15%People are clicking anywhere, usually "read more". The hook is working.
CTR link3% floor, 4% goodThe body copy or the video script is doing its job and moving them to the site.
Hook rate (video)creative levelHow many watched past 3 seconds. Judge per ad, never per account.
Hold rate (video)creative levelHow many stayed after the hook. Holds attention but no sale means the product or the value is missing, not the story.
Net new conceptsweekly countThe single best predictor of finding a winner. Gabriel's team: 10 a day.

The read, for static ads

PatternDiagnosisThe fix you recommend
High CTR all, low CTR linkImage and hook are pulling people in. The rest of the copy loses them.Keep the hook, rewrite the body. Easy win
Low CTR all, CTR link at targetEither the hook has obvious upside, or the copy speaks to a higher awareness level: fewer people, but warmer and easier to convert.New hooks on the same body, or deliberately widen to a lower awareness level. Easy win
Both lowThe concept is wrong, not the execution.New concept, not another variation. Real work
Both fine, no salesOffer or landing page, not the ad.Stop editing creative. Look at the page and the offer. Not a creative problem

Video reads the same way with hook rate and hold rate standing in for CTR all and CTR link.
