1. The split that actually matters

A no-brainer for one person is a chore for another. Some buyers want the value in their hands the second they pay. Booking a call, waiting for it, showing up at a set time, that is friction, and for them it makes the offer worse no matter how good the call is. Other buyers are stuck, know they are stuck, and a human hour is the single most valuable thing you could hand them.

We have been arguing about which of those to build. The answer is that they are two different buyers and we already know both of them are in our signup flow.

In value-equation terms this is precise, not a matter of taste. A scheduled call adds time delay and effort for someone who is capable and impatient, and removes far more of both for someone who is confused. Same item, opposite effect on value, depending on who is reading. That is why one price and one shape cannot serve both.

2. Version A: what we built

WeaknessIt is a second real purchase ninety seconds after the first. It needs a calendar. It caps at fifteen seats a week because there is one Vitaley. And Matt stopped reading at item four.

3. Version B: what Iman's model says

The $15 no-brainer
Shape$97 $15. Everything that costs us nothing to duplicate, unlocked the instant they pay. No call, no calendar, no waiting.
Best forThe capable operator who just wants the goods. Our log is full of them: people who self-taught, built their own pipelines, and only wanted the prompts and the filters.
StrengthAlmost nobody says no to $15 when it says $97 next to it. Zero marginal cost, so it scales without limit. And it still forces the activation actions, which is the entire point.
WeaknessIt hands the confused beginner a folder of assets, which is exactly what they already failed to use. It is a worse offer for the segment with the worst churn.

Per 1,000 signups. Take rates are assumptions, not measured and are the thing the first month of data replaces.

The break-even that settles it: for a $97-only page to beat a $15-only page on revenue, more than 3.9% of signups have to pay $97. Nobody in this company believes that number is comfortably clear. And on the metric that actually matters, the $15 activates eight times more people. Running both captures the operators who want a human without giving up the volume.

Cost side, so the numbers are honest: the $15 tier is pure assets, marginal cost around $4 in credits and infrastructure. The $97 tier costs an hour of Vitaley, which is why it is capped and priced where it is. The guarantee pays out in a free month, roughly $3.90, not in cash.

7. The one gap we cannot close ourselves

Iman's page is roughly 40% proof. Ours is 0%. Six named people with earnings circled, three prior events itemised, $55.6M aggregate. We have 68 logged calls, customers who upgraded live on the call, and Trustpilot reviews, and not one of them is on the page.

This needs three customers who agree to be named with a real number next to them. It is the highest-leverage single change available and it is the only one that requires something we do not already have. Starting it tonight matters more than any further copy revision.
